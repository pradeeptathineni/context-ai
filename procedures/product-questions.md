# Resolve an open product decision

Use only when a real product or scope decision is unresolved and the answer changes what gets built. Requirements the user already stated are settled; do not interview the user again or turn this into a planning phase. Hold scope: the questions narrow a decision, they do not add features.

Answer from available evidence first, then ask the user only the questions that evidence cannot settle, one line each with options and a recommendation:

1. **Demand reality**: who has this problem now, and what evidence shows it is real rather than hypothetical?
2. **Status quo**: what do they do today, and what does that cost them?
3. **Specificity**: which exact user, situation and outcome is this for? Replace vague terms with something measurable.
4. **Narrowest wedge**: what is the smallest version that delivers the outcome?
5. **Observation**: what did actual use or a trial show that was surprising?
6. **Future fit**: what change would make this choice wrong, and how cheaply can it be reversed?

Record the resolved decision and its reopening condition in the project's decision record, then continue the implementation.

The six question headings adapt gstack's office-hours product diagnostic (garrytan/gstack, MIT). Context excludes its founder-coaching posture, telemetry, browser tooling, persistence and hooks.
